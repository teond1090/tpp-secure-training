## Section 1 — Why TPP Secure Exists

First things first — and this one matters, so lean in. TPP Secure is a
protection plan. It is *not* insurance, and you should never call it
insurance. Why? The California Supreme Court settled this in Heckard versus
A-1 Self Storage: protection plans are not insurance and aren't regulated by
the Department of Insurance. So the words you use at the counter matter.
"Protection plan." Every time.

Now, about that two-thirds number. Sixty-seven percent of self-storage
tenants have no homeowners policy at all. And the tenants who *do* have
one? Many of their policies cap property stored away from home at just ten
to fifty percent of their contents limit — and some exclude stored property
completely. Most tenants have never read that page. They genuinely believe
they're covered. They're usually wrong.

That's the gap TPP Secure fills. When a covered loss happens, the claim is
processed based on fair market value — what the item is actually worth. And
here's a vocabulary moment, because these two get mixed up all the time:
"full replacement cost" would mean replacing the item with a brand-new one
of similar quality. That is *not* the same thing, and it's not how these
claims work. Fair market value — that's the phrase, and now you know the
difference, which puts you ahead of most people in this industry!

And unlike a homeowners claim, filing here never touches an insurance
premium. No deductible surprise on a homeowners policy, no rate creep for
the next three years. Just a plan that does what it says.

## Section 2 — Plans & Presenting

Let's talk plans — and more importantly, how to offer them.

The example levels are two thousand, three thousand, and five thousand
dollars of coverage. But listen — those are examples, not a fixed rate
card. Our coverage levels and pricing are flexible, and we can accommodate
most customer needs and requests. If a tenant needs something different?
Just ask. That's a normal conversation, not an exception.

One rule to remember: plans of five thousand dollars and above need a short
application with an inventory list and photos, done through the BirdsEye
tool. It takes minutes, and it means underwriters have a baseline before
anything ever happens.

Now — the fun part. How do you offer this without sounding like a
salesperson? Simple. Ask *which*, not *whether*.

"Would you like a protection plan?" — that invites a no. But "Which level
of protection would you like?" — now the conversation is moving! Name the
middle plan first, and you've got room to go up or down. And frame it
daily: nobody argues about fifty cents a day, but they'll argue about
fifteen dollars a month. Same number. Completely different conversation.

You're not selling. You're explaining a gap they don't know they have.
There's a difference, and tenants can feel it.

One more thing while we're here: the addendum. It records the coverage
level the tenant chose, and it's what every claim gets validated against.
Get it signed at the time of rental. Every tenant, every time, no
exceptions. Future-you will thank present-you.

## Section 3 — Coverage & Exclusions

Alright — what's actually covered? Fire, smoke, and explosion. Burglary.
Vandalism. Water damage from plumbing. Windstorm. Building collapse. And
rodent damage — capped at a five hundred dollar sublimit.

Now the exclusions, because these matter just as much. Three big ones:
flood — that's only available through the National Flood Insurance Program.
Mysterious disappearance — items that simply vanish with no evidence of
forced entry. And mold.

Burglary deserves its own moment, because there's a hard rule here: no
burglary claim is paid without a police report. Ever. The tenant files with
the police and provides a copy. The claim also needs photos of the visible
forced entry, and your incident report as the manager. So if a tenant calls
you about a break-in, the very first words out of your mouth: "Call the
police." Then take your photos and write your report.

Here's a skill that'll make you look brilliant: reading a declaration page.
When a tenant hands you their homeowners policy as proof of coverage, look
for one line — off-premises personal property. Many policies cap it at ten
to fifty percent of the contents limit, and some exclude it outright. If
you spot that exclusion, tell the tenant! Their belongings in your building
may not be covered at all, and they'd much rather hear it from you now than
from an adjuster after a loss.

And the tenant announcement letters? Easy. You approve them first — nothing
reaches a tenant until you've read it and signed off. Depending on your
program setup, we provide the letters finished and ready to send, or as
files your team uses to produce them. Either way, they go out from your own
property management software, so they arrive from a name your tenants
already recognize.

## Section 4 — Filing a Claim

Okay — something happened, and a tenant needs to file. Here's the play.

Claims are filed online at tppclaims.com, any hour of any day. The window
is thirty days from the date of *discovery* — not from when the damage
happened. That distinction matters when someone hasn't visited their unit
in a while.

Who does what? The tenant gathers their own receipts and documentation and
submits their own claim — that's their part. Your part is the incident
report and supporting paperwork. And tell tenants: don't move or clear
anything until the documentation is complete. Photos first, cleanup second.

Encourage them to open the claim right away and send documents in
afterwards — waiting until everything is gathered is exactly how people
miss the thirty-day window. And once a claim is open, keep every
conversation about it inside the claims portal, not email. Everything in
the portal lands in the claim file. Email is where details go missing.

What happens next? The moment a claim is submitted, a claim number is
issued and the tenant gets an automatic confirmation email — that's the one
automatic email in the process. After that, updates come from the adjuster
as the claim moves, and if you have claims portal access, you can check
status there any time.

Live adjusters are available Monday through Friday, seven A-M to five P-M
Arizona time. And when a tenant asks "when will I get paid?" — here's the
honest answer: processing can take up to thirty days, though many claims
are completed sooner. Never promise a payment date. And never, ever
estimate a reimbursement amount — even to be helpful! "I'm not the right
person to put a number on that, but the adjuster will walk you through it."
That's the perfect answer. Memorize it.
